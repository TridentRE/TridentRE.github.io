Investigation before investment

Understand the assembly.
Target the right work.

Commercial leak investigation connects interior water entry with roof conditions, repair history and the details above the affected space. Open seams, failed flashings, penetrations and drainage restrictions can produce similar symptoms inside; a surface patch alone may miss the source.

Trident uses core sampling where appropriate to identify roof layers, insulation thickness and signs of moisture at selected locations. Findings inform repair, removal and replacement decisions. Sample locations are properly resealed with compatible materials.

Core samples describe selected locations; findings are considered with the wider inspection when defining the scope.

  • Repairable detailsLocalised seam, flashing or penetration defects may allow targeted work where the surrounding assembly remains serviceable.
  • Broader deteriorationRepeated leaks, wet insulation, widespread membrane defects and failed previous repairs can change the extent of removal or replacement needed.
  • Capital planningCompare the affected area, remaining roof condition, drainage improvements and planned equipment changes so the scope addresses more than the latest leak.
Commercial roofing & building-envelope services

Keep water moving.
Keep the envelope connected.

Repair and replacement scopes need to address both the waterproofing and the route water takes off the building. Trident considers these connections together, including the changes introduced by rooftop equipment and exterior work.

Membrane repairs &
detail waterproofing

We repair open seams and laps, damaged field membrane, pipe boots, duct penetrations and deteriorated flashings. Curbs, parapets and roof-to-wall junctions need continuous flashing and counterflashing details compatible with the existing roof, rather than relying on exposed sealant as the whole repair.

Drains, scuppers &
positive drainage

We assess internal drains and sumps, scupper openings through parapets, overflow provisions and leader connections. Blockages, high drain edges or poorly directed slope can leave water behind. Tapered insulation and crickets can redirect flow around equipment and walls toward outlets; the layout must account for existing curb and perimeter heights.

HVAC & penetration
coordination

Equipment replacement and new services alter the roof's waterproofing. Trident coordinates the roofing interfaces with the mechanical trades: curb locations and heights, flashing tie-ins, supports, duct and pipe openings, and the closure of abandoned penetrations. The roofing scope addresses the weatherproof interface; mechanical connections and equipment servicing belong in the appropriate trade scope.

Roof-to-wall metal,
cladding & exterior edges

Coping and cap metal, wall flashings and cladding transitions must shed water over the layers below. We assess and repair loose metal, open joints and connections at parapets, siding, fascia and soffit. Eavestrough repairs and outlet connections also matter where the building drains at its edges.

Understanding commercial roof systems

Different assemblies.
Different detailing requirements.

The system name is only part of the decision. Building use, the existing assembly, insulation condition, attachment, drainage and compatibility with repairs all influence a suitable roofing scope. These common system families provide context for that discussion.

Thermoplastic single ply

TPO / PVC

Sheet membranes joined with heat-welded seams. Weld continuity and compatible flashings around curbs, penetrations and edges are central to waterproofing; TPO and PVC are distinct materials, not interchangeable repair products.

Rubber single ply

EPDM

A rubber membrane with system-compatible seam and flashing details. Existing seams, adhesive or tape connections and penetration flashings need assessment when planning repairs or alterations.

Reinforced asphalt

Modified bitumen

Reinforced asphalt sheets commonly arranged in base and cap assemblies. Laps, surfacing condition and transitions into flashings help determine how repairs tie into the existing roof.

Multi-ply asphalt

Built-up roofing (BUR)

Multiple layers of roofing ply and asphalt form the waterproofing assembly, with surfacing that varies by system. Assessment considers the layers below the surface as well as visible defects and flashing condition.

Preventative maintenance

Address developing defects.
Plan beyond the next leak.

Preventative maintenance focuses attention on the details that change over time. Clearing drainage paths and correcting developing defects helps owners manage roof condition before minor issues become larger repair scopes.

Trident's maintenance work is based on the building's condition, exposure, repair history and rooftop activity, with priorities that distinguish active water entry from defects requiring planned attention.

  • Drainage paths: remove debris at drains and scuppers, check strainers and review signs of ponding or obstructed outlets.
  • Waterproofing details: check seams, flashings, penetration seals and perimeter metal for movement, separation and deterioration.
  • Equipment interfaces: review roof damage or disturbed flashings around service access, supports and recent rooftop alterations.
  • Repair priorities: identify compatible local repairs, areas needing further investigation and conditions that should inform replacement budgeting.

Work around an occupied property. Agree on roof access, tenant communication, deliveries, equipment shutdowns and coordination with other trades before work. These requirements belong in the scope and scheduling discussion alongside the roofing details.

Owner & property-manager questions

Practical answers.
Better roof decisions.

Useful information for managing an existing commercial roof, planning equipment changes or evaluating recurring leaks.

Does a leaking commercial roof need full replacement?

Not always. A localised seam or flashing failure may suit a targeted repair. Widespread deterioration, wet insulation, recurring leaks and drainage deficiencies may justify a broader scope. Inspection and, where appropriate, core samples help establish the difference.

What does a roof core sample establish?

A core sample exposes the assembly's layers and insulation condition, helping verify what is beneath the membrane before defining work. The test cut is resealed with compatible materials.

How is roofing coordinated with HVAC replacement?

Trident coordinates the proposed curb and penetration changes with the mechanical trades before installation. Flashing tie-ins, support details, abandoned openings and shutdown requirements are addressed so the roof interface is included in the agreed scope.

Why does water remain around equipment or drains?

Debris, restricted outlets, high drain edges and inadequate slope can all contribute. Review the drainage route and available heights before selecting a correction. Tapered insulation, a sump or a cricket may help direct water, depending on the existing assembly.

What information helps with an inspection request?

Provide the property address, leak locations and timing, known roof age or system, previous repairs, equipment changes and safe photos. Include access or occupancy constraints. For storm-related damage, note the event date; insurance coverage and claim decisions remain with the insurer.
