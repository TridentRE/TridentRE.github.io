01 / Understand the issue

A leak is a symptom.
Start with the building.

Water appearing inside does not always identify where it entered. For business owners and property managers, the first decision is not simply which material to buy; it is understanding the affected area and how the roof and exterior connect.

Share when the leak occurs, whether it has returned after previous repairs and any changes you have noticed inside. These observations help focus the investigation rather than treating every roof concern as a full replacement.

  • Roof detailsConsider penetrations, transitions, flashings and seams, alongside visible surface condition and previous repair areas.
  • Water movementLook at drainage routes, roof edges and eavestrough concerns that may contribute to water collecting or escaping where it should not.
  • Interior observationsDiscuss stained ceilings, affected rooms and the timing of water entry alongside exterior findings. Access and the assessment scope are agreed for the property.
02 / Define the right scope

Commercial roofing.
The exterior around it.

A practical recommendation starts with the existing building. Roof condition, the size of the affected area, repeated leaks and drainage concerns all matter when weighing a targeted repair against more extensive work.

01Roof inspections & leak repairs

Investigate the concern and discuss the details that need attention. A repair scope should identify the area involved and the proposed work, while recognising that a local repair may not address deterioration elsewhere on the roof.

02Flat roof repair or replacement

A flat roof does not automatically need replacing because it leaks. Assess the existing condition, recurring issues and drainage before comparing options. The roof system, material suitability and whether Trident can undertake the proposed work must be confirmed for your property.

03Siding, soffit, fascia & eavestroughs

Discuss damaged or loose cladding, exposed trim, soffit concerns and eavestrough leaks or overflow. Consider how these details meet the roof edge and direct water away from the building, so the written scope addresses the relevant connections rather than isolated symptoms.

04Storm damage documentation

Discuss observed roof and exterior damage after a storm and the repair needs it raises. Information about visible damage may support an insurance discussion; coverage, claim requirements and decisions remain with your insurer. An assessment does not guarantee a claim outcome.

Before you book

Questions from
property owners.

Every building has different roof details, access needs and repair history. Start with the information you have.

Does a leak mean replacing the whole roof?

Not necessarily. A localised issue may suit a repair; widespread deterioration or repeated leaks may warrant a broader scope. The recommendation depends on the property's condition and assessment findings.

Can the building stay in use during work?

That depends on the proposed work and site conditions. Discuss occupancy, noise-sensitive activities, entrances and deliveries before agreeing on access and scheduling. Uninterrupted operations cannot be assumed.

What should I send with my inquiry?

Include the property address, photos taken from a safe location, where and when the issue appears, any known roof history and access constraints. Please do not climb onto the roof to gather information.

Which commercial roof systems do you work on?

Send any known roof-system or material details with your inquiry. Suitability and the work Trident can undertake are confirmed for each property; this page does not promise every membrane or system.

What if the concern follows a storm?

Share the date, visible damage and any interior water entry. Discuss assessment and documentation needs with Trident, and confirm claim requirements directly with your insurer.
